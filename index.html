Wien.

Eight places worth your time

Vienna blends imperial grandeur, quirky modern architecture, and tranquil natural spaces. Here is what each spot offers to help you decide.

01

Belvedere Palace

Schloss Belvedere

Imperial Baroque grandeur & iconic Austrian art

A UNESCO World Heritage complex comprising the Upper and Lower Belvedere set within stepped French formal gardens. The Upper Belvedere holds Austria's premier art collection, headlined by Gustav Klimt's The Kiss and major works by Egon Schiele.

Best for — anyone wanting classic Viennese palatial history and world-famous fine art.

02

Botanical Garden

Botanischer Garten der Universität Wien

An unhurried, leafy scientific oasis right in the city

Founded in 1754 by Empress Maria Theresa, this 8-hectare living laboratory borders the Belvedere gardens and cultivates over 12,000 plant species from across the globe. It is quiet, educational, and free to enter.

Best for — nature lovers and travelers seeking a quiet stroll away from tourist crowds.

03

Imperial Butterfly House

Schmetterlinghaus

A humid, tropical slice of rainforest inside a historic palace park

Located in a grand Art Nouveau glasshouse within the Hofburg's Burggarten, the sanctuary keeps a year-round temperature of 26 °C with 80% humidity. Around 400 colorful, free-flying tropical butterflies flutter among indoor waterfalls and exotic blossoms.

Best for — a quick, sensory visit, especially rewarding on cold or rainy days.

04

Palace of Justice

Justizpalast

Imposing Neo-Renaissance civic architecture & a local skyline secret

The working seat of Austria's Supreme Court. Visitors pass through security to enter the cavernous central atrium, which features a monumental marble staircase and three-story arcades. The 5th floor houses the rooftop Justiz-Café, which serves weekday meals and drinks alongside open terrace views across Vienna's roofs.

Best for — architecture fans and budget-friendly rooftop viewpoints.

05

Setagayapark

Japanese Garden in Döbling

Serene, meditative Japanese garden design in Vienna's 19th district

Built in 1992 by Japanese landscape architect Ken Nakajima to honor a twinning partnership with Setagaya, Tokyo. Packed into roughly 4,700 m² are stone pagodas, arched wooden bridges, cascading waterfalls, a tea house, and a pond with koi fish. Note: closed during winter, November through February.

Best for — quiet contemplation, Japanese aesthetics, and photo walks (especially during cherry blossom season or autumn foliage).

06

Kunst Haus Wien

Museum Hundertwasser

Whimsical, anti-conformist, and deeply ecological

Designed by Austrian eco-artist Friedensreich Hundertwasser, this building refuses straight lines and level floors. Expect uneven tiled corridors, patchwork ceramic facades, and live "tree tenants" growing straight out of windows. It houses Hundertwasser's paintings and architectural philosophy alongside rotating contemporary photography exhibits.

Best for — creatives, fans of unique architecture, and sustainable art enthusiasts.

Official site Google Maps ↗
07

Haus des Meeres

Aqua Terra Zoo

Vibrant marine & exotic wildlife inside massive WWII defense architecture

A public zoo installed inside a massive concrete flak tower in Esterházypark. Spanning 11 floors, it features a two-story shark tank, an aquarium walk-through tunnel, a multi-story tropical house with free-roaming monkeys and birds, and a 360° open-air rooftop observation deck.

Best for — animal lovers, families, and anyone intrigued by adaptive reuse of wartime history.

Official site Google Maps ↗
08

House of Music

Haus der Musik

Playful, high-tech auditory exploration in the historic capital of music

Housed in the palace where the Vienna Philharmonic was founded, this interactive museum turns sound physics and classical music into games. Highlights include the Virtual Conductor — where a projected Vienna Philharmonic plays to your baton tempo, or stops and heckles you if you lose time — and interactive sound installations.

Best for — interactive learners, music enthusiasts, and fun rainy-afternoon visits.
