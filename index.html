Nine places worth your time

Vienna blends imperial grandeur, quirky modern architecture, and tranquil natural spaces. Here is what each spot offers to help you decide.

01

Belvedere Palace

Schloss Belvedere

Baroque architecture, formal gardens and Austrian art

The Upper and Lower Belvedere and their French-style gardens are part of Vienna’s UNESCO-listed Historic Centre. The Upper Belvedere displays a major collection of Austrian art, including Gustav Klimt’s The Kiss and works by Egon Schiele.

Best for — art, Baroque architecture and a garden walk.

  • Admission — The main gardens are free; museum entry is paid. Upper and Lower Belvedere tickets have timed entry; booking online is recommended.
  • Suggested time — 2–3 hours for the Upper Belvedere and a stroll through the main gardens (estimate).
  • Address — Prinz-Eugen-Straße 27, 1030 Vienna. Current tickets, opening hours and access.

02

Botanical Garden

Botanischer Garten der Universität Wien

A free, leafy retreat adjoining the Belvedere gardens

Established in 1754 under Empress Maria Theresa, the University of Vienna’s garden holds more than 12,000 plant species from six continents. Its paths, meadows, specialist plant collections and greenhouses offer a quiet change of pace close to the city centre.

Best for — a relaxed garden walk, plant collections and a quiet break.

  • Admission — Free. Seasonal opening hours; the garden may close in severe weather.
  • Suggested time — 1–2 hours (estimate).
  • Entrance — Main visitor entrance at Mechelgasse 2, 1030 Vienna. Closed December 24–January 6. Check current opening hours and access.

Imperial Butterfly House

Schmetterlinghaus

A tropical glasshouse in the Hofburg’s Burggarten

Inside an Art Nouveau palm house at the edge of Burggarten, around 400 live tropical butterflies fly among exotic plants and waterfalls. Expect warm, very humid conditions year-round—a short, sensory visit that can be especially welcome on a cold or rainy day.

Best for — butterflies, tropical plants and a compact indoor stop.

  • Admission — Paid entry; see the official site for current ticket prices and hours.
  • Suggested time — 30–60 minutes (estimate).
  • Location — Burggarten, near the Hofburg and Vienna State Opera. Check visitor information before going.

Palace of Justice

Justizpalast

Neo-Renaissance architecture and a working courthouse

Austria’s Supreme Court is housed in this richly decorated courthouse. Visitors can see the central hall, grand staircase and second-floor gallery; the public areas are limited, so this is a brief architectural visit rather than a museum tour. The Justiz-Café occupies the fifth floor.

Best for — architecture enthusiasts and a weekday café stop with a view.

  • Admission — The designated public areas and café are accessible after a security check; other areas are not open to sightseeing visitors.
  • Suggested time — 30–60 minutes (estimate); café time is extra.
  • Access — Public areas are open Monday–Friday, 07:00–16:00; the café is open 08:00–16:00 and closed on weekends and public holidays. Security checks apply; leave knives, scissors and similar restricted items behind. Official visitor rules and public areas.

05

Setagayapark

Japanese Garden in Döbling

A small Japanese-style garden in Vienna’s 19th district

Planned in 1992 by Japanese garden designer Ken Nakajima, the park reflects the friendship between Döbling and Tokyo’s Setagaya district. Its roughly 4,700 m² include a tea house, a stone lantern, a watercourse, ponds and seasonal plantings.

Best for — a peaceful garden walk, especially during cherry blossom or autumn colour.

  • Admission — Free; opening and closing times change with the season.
  • Suggested time — 30–60 minutes (estimate).
  • Seasonal access — The City of Vienna’s English and German pages disagree about November and winter closure dates. Check the current hours before a late-autumn or winter visit: English · Deutsch.

06

Hundertwasserhaus

Hundertwasser House

Colourful, tree-filled residential architecture

Designed by artist Friedensreich Hundertwasser in collaboration with architects Josef Krawina and Peter Pelikan, this lively apartment building is known for its irregular lines, vivid façades and greenery woven into balconies and roof terraces. It is an inhabited residential house, so visitors admire it from the street only—please respect residents’ privacy. The Hundertwasser Village, a separate visitor attraction, is opposite; this is not the nearby Kunst Haus Wien museum.

Best for — a quick architectural stop, colourful street photography and fans of Hundertwasser’s work.

  • Admission — Free to view from the street; the residence is not open to visitors.
  • Suggested time — 20–30 minutes outside; allow longer if visiting the Village.
  • Address — Kegelgasse 34–38, 1030 Vienna. Vienna Tourist Board visitor information.

Haus des Meeres

Aqua Terra Zoo

A modern aquarium and zoo inside a former WWII flak tower

This zoo occupies a former anti-aircraft flak tower and spans 11 floors. Explore aquariums, the Atlantic tunnel, tropical habitats and more than 10,000 animals, then take in the city view from the rooftop. The recent glass addition is a striking counterpoint to the original concrete tower.

Best for — marine life, families and seeing how a historic tower has been adapted for a new use.

  • Admission — Paid zoo entry; online tickets can help avoid queues.
  • Suggested time — 2–3 hours (estimate).
  • Hours & access — Daily 09:00–20:00, including public holidays. Address: Fritz-Grünbaum-Platz 1, 1060 Vienna. Check tickets, visitor details and current hours.

08

House of Music

Haus der Musik

Interactive music, sound and science in a historic palace

Set in Palais Erzherzog Carl, where composer Otto Nicolai lived while founding the Vienna Philharmonic, this four-floor museum explores sound and Viennese classical music. Try the interactive sound lab, discover the Philharmonic’s history and take the conductor’s baton: a projected orchestra responds to your movements.

Best for — music fans, curious families and a lively indoor visit.

  • Admission — Paid museum entry; check the official site for current tickets.
  • Suggested time — 1.5–2.5 hours (estimate).
  • Hours — Daily 10:00–22:00; last admission 21:00. Check holiday hours before visiting.

09

Votive Church

Votivkirche

Neo-Gothic spires, stained glass and a quiet landmark near the Ring

Heinrich von Ferstel’s twin-spired basilica was built as a votive offering after Emperor Franz Joseph survived an assassination attempt in 1853. At 99 metres, it is Vienna’s second-tallest church after St. Stephen’s Cathedral, and its nave is lit by 78 stained-glass windows. The small oratory museum holds the late-medieval Antwerp Passion Altar. The Votivkirche is an active parish church—please visit quietly and avoid service times.

Best for — Gothic architecture, stained glass and a peaceful stop near the university quarter.

  • Admission — Church entry is free during visitor hours; the oratory museum costs €8 (€7 reduced; under 14 free).
  • Suggested time — 30–60 minutes (estimate).
  • Hours — Church: Tue–Fri 10:00–16:00, Sat 11:00–19:00, Sun and public holidays 09:00–13:00; closed Monday. Museum: Tue–Fri 13:00–15:00, Sat 14:00–17:00, or by appointment; reached by a spiral staircase, so not step-free. Church hours · Museum details.
  • Address — Straße des 8. Mai, 1090 Vienna. U2 Schottentor.
